Michigan Estate Planning Checklist

A short, plain-English starter. Three sections, no fluff — the things most families forget, and a few questions to answer before your first attorney meeting.

1. The five core documents

Most complete estate plans rest on five documents. If you have all five, properly executed under Michigan law, you've covered the major scenarios.

  • Last Will & Testament — names guardians for minor children, a personal representative, and directs distribution.
  • Revocable Living Trust — manages assets during life and avoids probate at death (when properly funded).
  • Durable Financial Power of Attorney — names someone to handle finances if you're incapacitated.
  • Patient Advocate Designation — Michigan's healthcare power of attorney.
  • HIPAA Authorization — lets your loved ones access your medical information.

2. Things people commonly forget

  • Trust funding. A trust only works for assets actually titled in its name. Most plans go wrong here, not in the drafting.
  • Beneficiary designations. Retirement accounts, life insurance, and bank PODs pass outside your will. Check them annually.
  • Digital assets. Email, photos, password manager, crypto, social accounts — make a list, and decide who can access what.
  • Pets. Michigan recognizes pet trusts. Even informally, name a caregiver and provide for their costs.
  • Out-of-state property. Real estate in another state can trigger separate (ancillary) probate. A trust solves this.
  • Letters of instruction. Non-binding, but enormously helpful for your family. Where things are, what your wishes are, who to call.

3. Questions to think about before your first attorney meeting

  • Who would you name as guardian for any minor children?
  • Who would you trust to make financial decisions for you?
  • Who would you trust to make medical decisions for you?
  • What matters most to you about how your estate is divided?
  • Are there family complications — second marriages, estranged relatives, special-needs beneficiaries — that need to be addressed?
  • What's your rough net worth, and where are your assets held?
  • Do you have long-term care concerns for yourself or a parent?
